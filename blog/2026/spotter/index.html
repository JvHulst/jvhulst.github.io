Building a workout app without knowing JavaScript

Created on November 11, 2026

2026

I’m a bit obsessed with data. I’ve been logging my workouts for over three years now, in an app called Strong, and there are over 500 workouts in there. Fitness apps always had a catch, though. The interesting features sat behind a premium subscription, or they worked almost, but not quite, the way I wanted.

So I made my own. Spotter is a free workout tracker for the iPhone that runs in the browser and does things exactly my way. Some of what it does:

  • I used scientific studies on lifting to build its suggestions for splits, exercise rotations and weight increases.
  • From your first set of an exercise last time, it estimates how strong you are and suggests the smallest step up: one more rep, or a slight increase in weight. It also knows when you haven’t done an exercise in a while and suggests restarting it slowly.
  • You can favorite exercises, and the app’s split templates always prioritize them.
  • It shows weekly and per-exercise figures and stats for those who are interested.
  • It imports the workout history that you can export from the Strong app. With minor tweaks, it can work with other apps too.
  • After eight weeks it rotates your exercises. When an exercise stalls, it also proposes a change.
  • Warm-ups on barbell exercises only ever add plates.
  • It stores all your data locally in your web browser, and you can export it to a CSV file. None of the data is shared with me.
  • You can sync your data between devices by setting up a private GitHub repository. The data is always encrypted.
  • I also had some fun adding unnecessary but amusing features. Find out for yourself ;)

The screenshots show made-up training data.

Building Spotter was also an experiment in how far AI-assisted coding can take you. I had never built a web app, and I had never really written JavaScript. Claude wrote nearly all of the code. My part was deciding what the app should do, trying each version in the gym and reporting what felt off.

To try it, open Spotter in Safari on your iPhone, tap Share and then Add to Home Screen. The code is on GitHub.
